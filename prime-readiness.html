The gates

Six gates a defence buyer checks

It is not about being bigger. A buyer has to show its own customer that its supply chain meets the requirements it passed down.

Registration: SAM.gov, UEI and CAGE

Free, and the gate that most often holds a shop up. SAM.gov gives you the UEI and the CAGE code a buyer looks you up by. It renews each year — let it lapse and you are off the books.

Your cybersecurity position

Where controlled data lives, who can reach it, and the evidence it meets the standard your contract names — NIST SP 800-171, written into defence contracts as CMMC. Other gates will not open until this one is answered.

Export control (ITAR)

Machine parts on the munitions list, or handle their drawings, and you register with the State Department and renew annually — including if you never export. The work is naming an owner, with a backup, and controlling how drawings move.

Quality

Proof your shop documents how it works and follows it. Aerospace and defence primes require the stricter version, and each special process — heat treat, welding, plating, inspection — needs its own accreditation. Buyers check it online, so a lapsed certificate counts for nothing.

What rides in on the purchase order

Clauses arrive with the order: protecting controlled data, keeping counterfeit parts out, tracing material, and where the metal was melted. For defence alloys that is settled before you quote — never after.

The buyer's own onboarding

Capability and quality questionnaires, a shop visit, its financial review, and a place on the approved supplier list — before the first order. A loan approval helps; it is not the prime's test.

Which gate bites first depends on the part and the customer. A commercial shop may need only the first; flight hardware, four.

Questions

What owners ask about this

Is this just CMMC?

No. It is the gate owners ask about most, and usually the largest, but it is one of about six. The map covers registration, export control, quality, purchase-order clauses and the buyer's own qualification too.

Do I need all of it?

It depends on the part, the customer and the contract. Some shops need one gate, some three. Working out which is what the map is for.

What does it cost?

The fee is agreed in writing before any work starts, and it is fixed rather than a percentage of anything. The map is a small piece of work at a fixed fee. The larger tracks, if you need them, are priced afterwards against the hours they take.

Will this get me a contract?

No one can promise that. The prime decides who it buys from. Answering its requirements makes your shop one a prime can consider.

Do I have to do this before I can get a loan?

No. Separate engagements, separate fees, neither depending on the other. A lender will ask where you stand on controlled data, so doing the map early can make the financing easier — but no loan requires it, and neither SBA nor any lender requires you to use us.
